Overview / 01

A commercial-grade successor to GridLAB-D, now stewarded in the open.

Arras began as GridLAB-D, developed for the U.S. Department of Energy by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory to study grid modernization. In 2018, the California Energy Commission funded SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory to harden it into HiPAS GridLAB-D — a commercial-grade simulation engine. LF Energy adopted the project in 2022 and renamed it Arras Energy, giving it a neutral, vendor-independent home for continued development.

History / 03

Two decades from research prototype to LF Energy project.

2002
PDSS demonstrated at PNNL
An agent-based solver technology demonstration lays the groundwork for the engine.
2007–2008
GridLAB-D funded and released
DOE funds development based on PDSS; the first free, open-source release ships in 2008.
2018
California Energy Commission funds HiPAS GridLAB-D
SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory builds a commercial-grade version under CEC grant EPC-17-046.
2022
LF Energy adopts the project as Arras
HiPAS GridLAB-D moves to a neutral open-source foundation under the name Arras Energy.
2025
v4.3.1 "chiba-1" and GridChat
A major release adds hosting-capacity, resilience, tariff, and electrification support — alongside GridChat, a GenAI copilot for modelers.
New this release / 04
GenAI copilot

GridChat

Built on AWS and powered by Amazon Bedrock, GridChat helps modelers navigate GLM syntax, troubleshoot simulations, and pull up-to-date documentation — without leaving the workflow. It's a copilot for the modeling language, not a replacement for the engine.
